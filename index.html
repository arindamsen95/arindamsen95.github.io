What I work on

I did my PhD in experimental high-energy physics, developing low-resistivity bakelite Resistive Plate Chambers for muon detection in the CBM experiment at FAIR — including a new linseed-oil coating technique now used across the collaboration's RPC R&D. Alongside that, I studied how cosmic-ray muon flux responds to atmospheric conditions using plastic scintillation detectors.

At Jefferson Lab I moved into data acquisition: setting up and integrating VXS-based counting DAQ test stands for the MOLLER experiment, and monitoring charge asymmetry and feedback across Hall A and Hall C during data taking. My current project at Jagiellonian University turns that detector background toward a different question — using inertial sensing on positronium atoms to measure gravitational effects on a matter–antimatter system.
